Cutoff marks explained

How JAMB and university cutoff marks work

"Cutoff mark" actually refers to two different numbers, and mixing them up is the most common mistake applicants make.

Tier 1

JAMB's national minimum

After every UTME, JAMB sets a national baseline score that all candidates need to even be considered for admission historically around 140–160 for universities and lower for polytechnics and colleges of education. This figure is announced fresh each admission cycle.

Tier 2

Institutional / departmental cutoff

Each university then sets its own, usually higher, cutoff per course often well above JAMB's minimum for competitive courses like Medicine, Law, and Pharmacy in popular schools. This is decided by the institution after JAMB's CAPS portal opens.

Tier 3

Post-UTME / DE screening

Most universities also run their own post-UTME or direct-entry screening (a test, an aggregate score, or O'level review) before finally admitting candidates through CAPS.

Important: always confirm the current figure yourself

Cutoff marks change every single admission cycle and differ by school and department. We deliberately don't print specific numbers here because a wrong figure could cost you an application. For the current, official cutoff mark for any school and course, check the JAMB CAPS portal (jamb.gov.ng) or that school's admissions page — or message us on WhatsApp and we'll help you look it up.

Course guide

JAMB subject combinations & study tips by course

English Language is compulsory for every combination below. Tap a course to see the full combination, typical O'level requirements, and how to actually prepare for it.

Medicine & Surgery (MBBS)+
JAMB combination

English Language, Physics, Chemistry, Biology.

O'level requirement

Credits in English, Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, and Biology — usually at one sitting for the most competitive schools.

Study tips
  • Prioritise Biology and Chemistry, they carry the most conceptual overlap with what you'll face in post-UTME screening.
  • Practice past questions under timed conditions weekly; Medicine cutoffs reward speed and accuracy, not just knowledge.
  • Build strong foundations in human biology topics (genetics, physiology) early, since they resurface throughout the course.
Nursing Science+
JAMB combination

English Language, Physics, Chemistry, Biology.

O'level requirement

Credits in English, Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, and Biology.

Study tips
  • Focus heavily on Biology, most Nursing post-UTME screenings weight it highest.
  • Practice diagrams and labelling questions; anatomy-style questions come up often.
  • Revise basic Chemistry calculations (concentrations, moles) since they show up in pharmacology-adjacent questions later.
Pharmacy+
JAMB combination

English Language, Physics, Chemistry, Biology.

O'level requirement

Credits in English, Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, and Biology.

Study tips
  • Chemistry is the backbone of Pharmacy, get very comfortable with organic chemistry and stoichiometry.
  • Don't neglect Physics; drug delivery and dosage-related questions often lean on basic physics concepts.
  • Pharmacy cutoffs tend to sit close to Medicine's, so treat your prep with the same intensity.
Medical Laboratory Science+
JAMB combination

English Language, Physics, Chemistry, Biology.

O'level requirement

Credits in English, Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, and Biology.

Study tips
  • Biology and Chemistry questions dominate; drill microbiology and basic biochemistry topics from the WAEC syllabus.
  • Pay attention to laboratory-technique style questions, they appear more here than in other biology-heavy courses.
Law (LL.B)+
JAMB combination

English Language, Literature-in-English, Government (or CRS/IRS), and one other Arts/Social Science subject.

O'level requirement

Credits in English, Literature-in-English, Government, and Mathematics are typically required.

Study tips
  • English Language and Literature carry real weight, read widely and practice comprehension and essay-style questions.
  • Law is one of the most competitive courses nationally; a strong Government or CRS/IRS score is often the deciding factor.
  • Follow current affairs; many Government questions and post-UTME essays draw on recent civic and legal issues.
Computer Science+
JAMB combination

English Language, Mathematics, Physics, and one of Chemistry, Biology, Agricultural Science, Geography, or Economics.

O'level requirement

Credits in English, Mathematics, and Physics are essential; a fourth science or social science subject completes the set.

Study tips
  • Mathematics is the single biggest lever for your score, master algebra, functions, and logic-based questions.
  • Physics questions tend to focus on electricity and basic mechanics; prioritise those topics if time is short.
  • Many schools now run a coding-aptitude or logic screening at post-UTME stage, practising basic logical reasoning helps.
Electrical / Electronic Engineering+
JAMB combination

English Language, Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry.

O'level requirement

Credits in English, Mathematics, Physics, and Chemistry.

Study tips
  • Mathematics and Physics together decide most of your score, give them the majority of your revision time.
  • Focus your Physics revision on electricity, magnetism, and waves; they're the most tested topics for this course.
Mechanical & Civil Engineering+
JAMB combination

English Language, Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry.

O'level requirement

Credits in English, Mathematics, Physics, and Chemistry.

Study tips
  • Build strong mechanics and force/energy fundamentals in Physics; they underpin most engineering coursework later.
  • Practice applied Mathematics questions (mensuration, trigonometry, vectors) since they mirror early coursework.
Accounting+
JAMB combination

English Language, Mathematics, Economics, and one of Financial Accounting, Commerce, or Government.

O'level requirement

Credits in English, Mathematics, Economics, and a commercial subject such as Financial Accounting or Commerce.

Study tips
  • Mathematics accuracy matters most here, practice speed drills on ratios, percentages, and basic algebra.
  • Get comfortable with accounting terminology early if your school offers it; it reduces first-year culture shock.
Economics+
JAMB combination

English Language, Mathematics, Economics, and one other Social Science subject (Government, Geography, or Accounting).

O'level requirement

Credits in English, Mathematics, and Economics.

Study tips
  • Mathematics underpins Economics scoring more than most students expect, don't neglect it in favour of theory.
  • Understand core concepts (demand/supply, inflation, national income) deeply rather than memorising definitions.
Mass Communication+
JAMB combination

English Language and any three of Government, Literature-in-English, CRS/IRS, Economics, or another Arts/Social Science subject — combination varies by school.

O'level requirement

Credits in English Language plus at least one relevant Arts or Social Science subject.

Study tips
  • English comprehension and essay-writing carry the most weight, read newspapers and practice summarising articles.
  • Stay current on national and global news; many post-UTME screenings for this course test general knowledge.
Microbiology & Biochemistry+
JAMB combination

English Language, Physics, Chemistry, Biology.

O'level requirement

Credits in English, Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, and Biology.

Study tips
  • Chemistry (especially organic chemistry) and Biology are weighted heavily budget more revision time there than Physics.
  • Practice diagram-based and process-sequence questions; they're common in this subject area.
Architecture+
JAMB combination

English Language, Mathematics, Physics, and one of Chemistry, Geography, or Fine Art.

O'level requirement

Credits in English, Mathematics, Physics, and one relevant fourth subject; many schools also assess a portfolio or drawing test.

Study tips
  • Mathematics accuracy (especially geometry and mensuration) matters for both UTME and school-level design tests.
  • If you can, start a basic sketching/drawing habit early, many schools test spatial reasoning at screening stage.
Psychology+
JAMB combination

English Language, Biology, and two of Chemistry, Physics, Mathematics, Economics, or Government — exact combination varies by school.

O'level requirement

Credits in English, Mathematics, Biology, and two other relevant subjects.

Study tips
  • Check your target school's exact combination early. Psychology requirements vary more across schools than most courses.
  • Biology fundamentals (especially nervous system topics) give you a head start once you resume.
Agricultural Science+
JAMB combination

English Language, Chemistry, Biology (or Agricultural Science), and Mathematics or Physics.

O'level requirement

Credits in English, Mathematics, Chemistry, and Biology or Agricultural Science.

Study tips
  • Biology and Agricultural Science overlap heavily, strong grades in one usually support the other.
  • Cutoffs for this course tend to be more moderate than Medicine or Engineering, but don't underestimate the Chemistry component.
Banking & Finance+
JAMB combination

English Language, Mathematics, Economics, and one of Accounting, Government, or Commerce.

O'level requirement

Credits in English, Mathematics, and Economics.

Study tips
  • Mathematics speed and accuracy is the biggest score driver, drill percentages, interest, and ratio-based questions.
  • Basic financial literacy concepts (interest rates, inflation) give useful context for both UTME and first-year courses.
